# lit-review

A 30-day, self-run trial of PhD-style research in **applied AI × distributed systems**, and the public record of the literature it covers.

## Why this exists

1. **To test fit.** Before committing years to a PhD, I want evidence on two questions:
   - Does the day-to-day *nature* of research work (slow reading, ambiguity, failure, self-direction) suit me?
   - Am I genuinely interested in applied AI and distributed systems, or only in the idea of them?
2. **To show the work.** Every paper I read, every research question I tried and dropped, and every failed experiment is kept here. It's evidence of how I think, not just a list of what I've read.

The experiment design is in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md). My personal reflections and the pass/fail criteria are kept in a private journal, so that writing them for an audience doesn't change them.

## How AI is used here

I use Claude Code as a **supervisor and reviewer**, not as an author. It scaffolds files, fetches citation metadata, explains concepts when I ask (like a labmate would), and critiques my work harshly. It does not write paper notes, synthesis, research questions or decks. Every critique it gives is committed next to my response in `sprints/*/review.md`.
